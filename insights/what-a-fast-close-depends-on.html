What a fast close really depends on

Actuals by Day 4 come from discipline before month-end, and that discipline makes AI easier to use later.

For several years I ran monthly cycles with actuals due by Day 4 across a cluster of more than 15 countries. People often assume a fast close is about working harder in the first days of the month. In my experience, most of it is decided before month-end begins.

Move the work earlier

Anything that can be known before the close should be finished before the close. Accrual estimates, trade spend and marketing commitments, and inventory checks are all easier when they are reviewed during the month. Teams that wait for the ledger to close are always fighting the calendar.

Agree what is material

A fast close needs a shared view of what matters. Chasing a small difference for two days adds little value. Clear materiality thresholds let people stop and move on, and they stop every market from setting its own standard.

Use one calendar and one template

With many markets, consistency matters more than any single clever report. One close calendar, one reporting template and one definition for each line mean that a question from a regional leader gets the same answer from everyone.

Review balance sheets, too

A close that looks only at the P&L stores up problems for later. Regular review of accruals and balance sheet movements is what stops a surprise arriving at year-end or during an audit.

Why this matters for AI

A disciplined close produces clean, consistent, well-labelled data on a predictable date. That is what automation needs. If your process depends on one person remembering an adjustment, a tool will not fix it, and may hide it. Standardise first, and AI will have something reliable to work with.

The result of a good close is time. The real measure is how much of the month your team spends explaining the numbers and how little it spends assembling them.
