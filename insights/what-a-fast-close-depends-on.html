What a fast close really depends on

Actuals by Day 4 come from discipline before month-end, and that discipline makes AI easier to use later.

For several years I ran monthly cycles with actuals due by Day 4 across a cluster of more than 15 countries. People often assume a fast close is about working harder in the first days of the month. In my experience, most of it is decided before month-end begins.

Move the work earlier

Anything that can be known before the close should be finished before the close. Accrual estimates, trade spend and marketing commitments, and inventory checks are all easier when they are reviewed during the month. Teams that wait for the ledger to close are always fighting the calendar.

Agree what is material

A fast close needs a shared view of what matters. Chasing a small difference for two days adds little value. Clear materiality thresholds let people stop and move on, and they stop every market from setting its own standard.

Use one calendar and one template

With many markets, consistency matters more than any single clever report. One close calendar, one reporting template and one definition for each line mean that a question from a regional leader gets the same answer from everyone.

Review balance sheets, too

A close that looks only at the P&L stores up problems for later. Regular review of accruals and balance sheet movements is what stops a surprise arriving at year-end or during an audit.

A close calendar that works

A Day 4 close is a sequence, not a sprint. The calendar below is an example of how the work can be spread so that nothing important waits for the ledger to close. The exact days will differ by company, but the principle holds: the more that happens before month-end, the calmer the first days of the new month.

How to get from Day 8 to Day 4

Few teams can halve their close in one step, and trying usually creates errors that take longer to fix than the days saved. A steadier path has four stages, each of which pays back on its own.

1

Map the current close

Write down every task, who does it, how long it takes and what it waits for. Most teams discover that the close is long because of waiting, not because of work.

2

Remove the waits

Move every task that does not need the closed ledger into the last week of the month. Replace waiting for documents with estimates that are reversed and corrected.

3

Agree materiality and standards

Set thresholds for investigation, and one template and one set of definitions for every market. This is where most of the remaining days disappear.

4

Standardise, then automate

Once the process is stable, automate the repetitive steps: reconciliations, recurring journals, data collection and first-draft commentary.

The order matters. Automating a close that still depends on waiting and rework simply produces the same delays faster, and makes them harder to see.

What the business gets from a faster close

A faster close is not an end in itself, and it is worth being clear about the benefit when asking colleagues to change their habits. The first gain is earlier decisions: a problem visible on day 4 can be acted on in the same month, while one seen on day 10 usually waits for the next. The second is trust. When numbers arrive on the same day every month and rarely change afterwards, leaders stop building their own shadow reports. The third is capacity. Every day taken out of the close is a day the finance team can spend with the business instead of on the ledger.

Common bottlenecks, and what fixes them

  • Waiting for invoices. Estimate and reverse instead. A good accrual on day 2 beats an exact invoice on day 9.
  • Intercompany mismatches. Agree balances between entities before month-end, not after both sides have closed.
  • Manual journals. Use standard templates, clear approval limits and recurring entries where the logic does not change.
  • Late commercial data. Agree cut-off rules with sales and marketing, so promotions and allowances are known before the close starts.
  • Single points of failure. If one person knows how an adjustment works, write it down and train a backup.

Why this matters for AI

A disciplined close produces clean, consistent, well-labelled data on a predictable date. That is what automation needs. If your process depends on one person remembering an adjustment, a tool will not fix it, and may hide it. Standardise first, and AI will have something reliable to work with.

The result of a good close is time. The real measure is how much of the month your team spends explaining the numbers and how little it spends assembling them.
