Gross-to-net, explained for people outside finance

A plain-English guide to the gap between list price and the money a company actually keeps.

Commercial teams often talk about gross sales. Finance talks about net revenue. The space between the two is called gross-to-net, and in consumer goods it can be very large. Understanding it is one of the fastest ways for anyone in a commercial role to have more influence on profit.

What sits in the gap

Gross sales are the value of what you ship at list price. From that, you deduct allowances: the discounts, promotions, rebates and other terms agreed with customers. What is left is net revenue, the money the business actually earns from the sale.

Why one point matters

Allowances are usually measured as a percentage of gross sales. One point sounds small, but it comes straight off net revenue, and most of it falls through to profit because the product cost has not changed. That is why finance watches this line so closely. Why it is hard to manage

  • It builds up over time. Each deal looks reasonable alone, and the total drifts upward.
  • It varies by channel. A domestic sale and a direct import or FOB sale carry very different terms, so channel mix moves the average.
  • It can be invisible. Some terms sit off-invoice, so the price on the invoice does not show the full picture.

Three questions worth asking

  1. What do we get in return for each allowance? A term linked to performance is easier to justify than one that has simply always been there.
  2. Is this rate above or below similar customers and markets?
  3. Has the change in our rate come from real terms, or only from a shift in mix?

When sales and finance read this line together, conversations about pricing improve quickly. It is one of the areas where a finance partner can add the most value to a commercial team.
